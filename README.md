# Typly

> Offline-first typing-exam practice for Indian competitive exams.

Typly helps aspirants for **SSC, Railway, Banking, Court, Stenography, Data Entry and
State** exams build real typing speed and accuracy under exam-accurate conditions — in
the browser or as a desktop app, and fully offline. Bring any passage (paste, image, PDF
or DOCX), practise against the exact scoring your exam uses, and track your progress
over time.

---

## Features

### Exam-accurate practice
- Net & gross **WPM**, **accuracy**, and per-mistake analysis matching real exam scoring.
- **Key depressions per hour (KDPH)** — the measure DEST and Data Entry Operator posts
  are actually recruited on. 8,000 and 15,000 KDPH profiles, a live depression counter,
  and every keystroke counted the way the notification counts it: corrections included.
- Built-in **exam profiles** — SSC (CHSL/CGL/MTS/DEST/DEO), Stenographer Grade C & D,
  Railway, Banking, Court, State & more.
- **Dictation → transcription** for Stenographer posts: the passage is *read aloud* at a
  controlled 80 or 100 words a minute and is never shown on screen, then the
  transcription window starts. Play, pause and repeat, with the pace held to the target
  by the clock rather than by the voice.
- **Exam mode** with optional exam lock, backspace/space/enter rules, and fullscreen.
- **Strict mode** blocks progress until the current word is correct — the standard cure
  for typing fast and fixing later, which is exactly what fails an accuracy-gated test.
- **Mock exam** flow: an instructions briefing, a reading window before the clock starts,
  and a **cut-off report** showing exactly how far each metric sits from the pass mark.
- **Pre-flight checks** in the briefing: Caps Lock, an OS input method that would fight
  InScript, a Devanagari font selected with nothing uploaded for it. Each of these
  silently ruins an attempt, and each is caught before the clock exists.
- An **exam-software skin** that imitates the real test client — candidate header, boxed
  passage above a plain input, the countdown where it really sits — because candidates
  panic over the interface, not the rules.
- **Multi-section mock papers** for CPCT and state exams: English and Hindi chained into
  one sitting with a single combined report, including the language switch where marks
  are really lost.
- An interrupted attempt is **checkpointed** — a reload or crash resumes it.

### Pace yourself against the real thing
- **Cut-off pacer** — a marker advancing at exactly the exam's minimum speed. Fall behind
  it and you would have failed. Needs no prior attempt, so it works on your first run.
- **Ghost race** — run against a past attempt of the same paragraph, live, as you type.
- **Pressure mode** — a flashing clock near the end, ambient hall noise and a live rank
  ticker. People lose 5–8 WPM to nerves on the day and otherwise cannot practise it.
- **Endless run** — passages keep coming, difficulty climbs while you hold the target and
  eases when you drop, and the run ends when you have missed the cut-off three times in a
  row. The output is one honest number: how long you can hold exam pace.

### Bring your own text
- A **sample paragraph** is in your library from the first launch.
- **Paste** a paragraph, or import from **image (OCR)**, **PDF**, or **DOCX**.
- On-device OCR (Tesseract) with an optional AI vision pass for tough scans.
- **Passage difficulty ratings** with matched recommendations — every paragraph scored on
  what actually slows typing down (word length, punctuation, capitals, digits, rare
  letters), and a note when one sits above or below your current level.
- **Bundled passage packs**: Indian polity, economy, an editorial and a real
  office-memorandum format, in English and Hindi. You are sitting a general-knowledge
  paper too, so an hour of typing may as well be an hour of reading what you need.
- **Scanned forms become data-entry tests**: OCR a printed register and Typly offers to
  recover its columns as Tab-separated fields, so it runs as a real DEST-style drill
  scored in key depressions rather than as one long paragraph.
- **English, Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Tamil and Gujarati**, with InScript, phonetic and
  Remington GAIL input for Devanagari.

### Learn & improve
- A **beginner → advanced lessons** curriculum that unlocks as you hit targets.
- **Custom lessons** — author your own drills from any text, with categories and targets.
- **Trainer** with two focuses: *accuracy* (weak keys, confused pairs, missed words,
  mistake kinds) and *speed* (per-key latency, slowest transitions, rhythm consistency,
  finger load) — each generating its own targeted drill.
- **Practice** drills for rows, numbers, symbols, the numpad, real editor shortcuts, and
  a **tabular data-entry drill**: roll numbers, names, dates and amounts, Tab-separated,
  numpad-heavy — the work a DEST or DEO post is actually for.

### Understand what went wrong
- **Mistake taxonomy** — mistakes split into transposition (`teh`), doubling, omission,
  substitution, shift and spacing errors, each with its own fix. The pattern is the
  actionable half; the word list is the evidence.
- **The cost of backspace** — "corrections cost you 47 seconds, about 4 WPM", measured
  from your own keystroke timing rather than assumed. The single most fixable habit at
  30–40 WPM.
- **Finger load and travel** — presses, distance and error rate per finger, so pinky
  overload or a right hand doing 60% of the work becomes visible. That is the mechanics
  behind a plateau, which no amount of "practice more" fixes.
- **Replay** any attempt keystroke by keystroke to see where the time actually went.

### Track your progress
- **Exam countdown with a readiness forecast** — set "SSC CHSL · 12 Nov" and the dashboard
  reads *68 days left · net 31 WPM against 35 needed · on this trend you clear it around
  20 Oct*. Pure local arithmetic over your own history.
- **Which post could you clear?** Your whole history re-scored against every exam
  profile at once — which posts you already pass (and whether repeatably, not by luck),
  which are one or two WPM away, and which are out of reach for now. Only attempts in an
  exam's own language count towards it.
- **A 30-day weak-key heatmap** compared against the 30 days before it, so a key you have
  been drilling shows whether it is actually healing.
- **A fatigue curve** — first-minute against last-minute WPM across attempts. Most
  aspirants fail a 10-minute DEST on fade, not on peak speed.
- **A monthly recap** — hours practised, WPM gained, keys fixed, best day, longest streak.
- History, per-minute WPM charts, cross-test progress, **badges**, **streaks** and a
  **daily goal**.

### Share and compete
- A **shareable result card** — a square image with WPM, accuracy, streak and the exam
  name, sized for WhatsApp and Instagram.
- A formal **certificate** for a passing attempt, printable or downloadable.
- **Challenge files** — export a passage plus your score as a small `.typly` file. A
  friend opens it, types the same passage under the same rules and the same clock, and
  gets a head-to-head report. Peer competition with no account and no server.

### For coaching centres
- **Institute branding**: your centre's name, logo and signatory on every certificate.
- **Batch certificates** — name each candidate against their passing attempt and generate
  the whole batch in one pass from the results list.

### Tools
- A **Kruti Dev ⇄ Unicode converter**, offline. Every Hindi typist needs it, and the
  alternative is pasting an official document into whichever website ranks first.
- A **keyboard health check** — press every key once and the app flags dead, sticky and
  ghosting keys. Worth two minutes when the machine is a lab or café PC you do not own.

### Language tools
- On-device **grammar** (Harper) and **spell-check** (nspell / low-memory fuzzy match).
- Optional **AI grammar** for English *and* Hindi.

### Optional AI — bring your own key
- An **AI coach** that turns each result into a personalised, targeted practice plan.
- A single master **Enable AI** switch; with AI off, everything falls back to the
  built-in offline pipeline. Your key is stored locally and only ever sent to the AI
  provider you choose.

### Built for focus, and for a long haul
- Text-to-speech, an on-screen **keyboard visualisation**, **blind mode**, and a clean,
  distraction-free UI.
- **System tray and taskbar** integration on desktop: today's practice at a glance, the
  daily reminder, quick actions, and live exam progress on the icon.
- A **60-second drill from the tray or a global hotkey** (`Ctrl`/`Cmd`+`Shift`+`T`) in a
  small always-on-top window. It saves like any other attempt, so it counts towards your
  streak — which is the point: a session that costs one keystroke to start is the kind
  that survives a busy evening.
- **20-20-20 eye breaks and a wrist prompt** during long sessions, never during a run.
  Two hours a day for eight months is where typing injuries come from.
- **Do not disturb** holds every notification — including the daily reminder, which is
  remembered rather than switched off — from Settings or straight from the tray.
- **Interface in English or Hindi**, switchable from the sidebar as well as Settings.

### Move between your own devices
- **Pair over Wi-Fi**: the desktop app shows a QR code, the other device scans it and
  pulls (or pushes) your tests, paragraphs and settings. No account, no server — the two
  devices talk directly, and pairing closes itself after a few minutes.

## Platforms

| Platform | Format |
| --- | --- |
| **Web / PWA** | Runs in any modern browser; installable, works offline |
| **Windows** | `Typly-<version>-x64.exe` · `-arm64.exe` (NSIS installer) · `-portable.exe` |
| **macOS** | `Typly-<version>-arm64.dmg` |
| **Linux** | AppImage · `.deb` · `.tar.gz` |

Desktop builds store your data in a local SQLite database; the web app uses your
browser's local storage. Nothing leaves your device unless you enable AI.

### Portable mode

The Windows portable build and the Linux tarball keep their database **beside the
executable**, in a `typly-data` folder, instead of in your user profile. Copy the folder
to a USB stick and it runs on a coaching-lab or cyber-café PC with no install rights —
and your practice history travels with you rather than being left on a shared machine.

Any build can be forced into portable mode with `--portable` (or `TYPLY_PORTABLE=1`); if
the directory turns out not to be writable, the app falls back to the user profile rather
than losing a session.

## Privacy

Typly is **offline-first**. Your practice text, results and settings stay on your
device. AI features are entirely optional and **bring-your-own-key** — the app works
fully without them.

Device sync keeps that promise literally: pairing serves your backup on your own local
network, guarded by a one-time token, for a few minutes — nothing is uploaded anywhere.
The Kruti Dev converter, OCR, grammar and spell-check all run on-device, which matters
when the text being converted is an official document.

## Tech

React 19 · TypeScript · Vite · Tailwind CSS v4 · Zustand · Electron · better-sqlite3 ·
Harper.js (WASM grammar) · Tesseract.js (OCR) · pdf.js · Bun.

### How the code is laid out

```
src/core/        Framework-free logic. Pure, testable, no React import anywhere.
  scoring/       WPM, KDPH, difficulty and mode modifiers, exam profiles, cut-offs
  analysis/      Mistake taxonomy, finger load, backspace cost, longitudinal trends
  exam/          Pacer, pressure, pre-flight, adaptive runs, papers, eligibility
  dictation/     Chunking and pacing a spoken passage
  text/          Extraction, cleanup, keymaps, Kruti Dev conversion, difficulty
  share/         Result-card rendering and the .typly challenge format
  institute/     Branding and certificate drawing
src/components/  Presentational React, grouped by the screen that uses them
src/hooks/       Stateful glue between core and components
src/pages/       One file per route
src/platform/    Ports (interfaces) plus a browser and an Electron implementation
electron/        Main process: window, tray, dock, SQLite store, portable paths
```

Every user-visible string lives in `src/i18n/en.ts`, and `hi.ts` is typed as a
*complete* record of its keys — so a new feature cannot ship without its Hindi wording.
