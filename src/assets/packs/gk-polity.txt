The Constitution of India came into force on the twenty-sixth of January, nineteen fifty, and remains the longest written constitution of any sovereign nation. It establishes a parliamentary system with a bicameral legislature at the centre, comprising the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha, and vests executive authority formally in the President while real authority rests with the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.

The Preamble declares India a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic and commits the state to securing justice, liberty, equality and fraternity for all its citizens. Part Three enumerates the Fundamental Rights, which are enforceable in the courts, while Part Four sets out the Directive Principles of State Policy, which guide the making of law without themselves being justiciable.

The Union Public Service Commission, established under Article three hundred and fifteen, conducts examinations for appointments to the all-India services. The Comptroller and Auditor General audits the accounts of the Union and of the states, and reports to Parliament through the President. The Election Commission superintends the conduct of elections to Parliament, to the state legislatures, and to the offices of President and Vice-President.

Amendments to the Constitution are made under Article three hundred and sixty-eight. The Supreme Court held in nineteen seventy-three that while Parliament may amend any provision, it may not alter the basic structure of the Constitution itself. That doctrine has since been applied to questions of federalism, judicial review, secularism and the conduct of free elections.
