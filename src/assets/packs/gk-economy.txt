The Reserve Bank of India was established in nineteen thirty-five and nationalised in nineteen forty-nine. It functions as the monetary authority, the regulator of the banking system, the manager of foreign exchange and the issuer of currency. Since twenty sixteen, monetary policy has been set by a six-member Monetary Policy Committee, which is required to keep retail inflation within a band of four per cent, plus or minus two percentage points.

The Goods and Services Tax replaced a long list of central and state indirect taxes in July twenty seventeen. It is levied at multiple rates and is administered jointly through the GST Council, on which the Union and every state are represented. Revenue from the integrated levy on inter-state supply is apportioned between the centre and the consuming state.

Financial inclusion has been pursued through the Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana, which opened basic savings accounts on a very large scale, and through the Unified Payments Interface, which settles retail payments instantly between bank accounts. Direct benefit transfers now deliver subsidies and pensions into those accounts, reducing leakage in delivery.

India's gross domestic product is measured at both constant and current prices, with the base year revised periodically by the Central Statistics Office. The Economic Survey is tabled in Parliament shortly before the Union Budget, and the Finance Commission recommends the formula by which central taxes are devolved to the states for a five-year period.
